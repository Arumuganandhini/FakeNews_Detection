# Notes on how the abstract was written

**Not part of the submission.** This records the guidance the abstract in
`abstract-simple.md` was written to, so the choices can be defended if a
reviewer questions the format, and so the same rules can be reapplied when the
abstract is revised.

| Rule followed | Source |
|---|---|
| One paragraph of 150–300 words | George Mason University Writing Center |
| Carries elements of introduction, method, results and discussion | George Mason University Writing Center |
| States findings, not intentions — no "this paper will examine" | George Mason University Writing Center |
| Gives specific results rather than vague categories | George Mason University Writing Center |
| Most important information first; clear, concise language | UNC Chapel Hill Writing Center |
| Does not refer extensively to other works | UNC Chapel Hill Writing Center |
| No citations, footnotes, equations or tables inside the abstract | IEEE Author Center style guidance |
| Self-contained and free of abbreviations | IEEE Author Center style guidance |
| Between 150 and 250 words | IEEE Author Center style guidance |
| Drafted after the full report existed | George Mason University Writing Center |

## Two judgement calls worth recording

**Naming the dataset.** The writing-centre guidance asks for specifics; the IEEE
rule forbids abbreviations. The submitted abstract resolves this by reporting the
figures without naming the corpus, keeping the paragraph self-contained; the
corpus is identified in the report itself.

**Where the research references sit.** IEEE requires the abstract paragraph to
carry no citations, so the references are listed after it rather than within it.
All are from 2023 onward, in line with the requirement that comparative work be
recent.
